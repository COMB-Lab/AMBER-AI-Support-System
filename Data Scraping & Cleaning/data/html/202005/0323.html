I now have a suitable test system with CUDA 10.2 and a properly supported
RTX-2080Ti that reproduces both the original Amber18 benchmarks and the
slowdown (about 14% from the first systems I've tested) in Amber20.
Furthermore, the results reflect my earlier findings of a ~5% speed
improvement in Amber20 over Amber18 for the older Pascal architecture. So
I definitely have a handle on the problem, now.

I also have a script comparing each of the compilations w.r.t. register
usage, which would have been my first guess as to what is wrong. While the
results are striking, it's not immediately obvious that Amber20 is overall
worse with respect to register spills than Amber18--indeed all of the
spills tend to happen in kernels that are infrequently called, and in many
cases Amber20 is significantly better in this regard (by my own
intention). I will continue to investigate this and expect to have a patch
soon, but for now please just be advised that there is a known slowdown in
the Turing (RTX) architecture relative to Amber18.

Dave
