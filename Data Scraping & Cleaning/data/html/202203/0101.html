Hi Matthew,

That's an easy fix! You just have to drop the last pipe
awk -F"_" '$1=$1' OFS=" " test | awk -F"." '$1=$1' OFS="\t" | awk '{print $2,$5,$7}'
To break it down, the first awk says find every _ and replace with a blank space, then does the same thing for @, and then finally just prints out the leftover cols 2, 5, and 7. The raw output after you get rid of the _ and @ (which is the harder part for other things to read in/deal with) in the first two awk passes would be
ASP 85 OD2 THR 66 OG1 0.9946
ASP 85 OD1 GLY 87 N 0.9598
ASP 85 OD2 GLN 79 N 0.5442
ASP 85 OD1 THR 66 N 0.1769
ASP 85 O TRP 88 N 0.0670
ASP 85 OD2 GLN 79 NE2 0.0563
ASP 85 OD2 LEU 78 N 0.0107
ASP 85 OD1 THR 66 OG1 0.0027
awk -F"_" '$1=$1' OFS=" " test | awk -F"@" '$1=$1' OFS="\t" | awk '{print $2,$5,$7}'
​85 66 0.9946
85 87 0.9598
85 79 0.5442
85 66 0.1769
85 88 0.0670
85 79 0.0563
85 78 0.0107
85 66 0.0027
Just comes from printing the relevant portions (col 2, 5, and 7 unless you want the atoms involved, that is).

awk -F"_" '$1=$1' OFS=" " test | awk -F"." '$1=$1' OFS="\t" | awk '{print $2,$5,$7}' | awk '!_[$1,$2]++’
What the last bit does is keep only the first instance of any pattern based on 1+2 of the residue# residue# format; I forgot in my case I wasn't looking for redundant Hbonds, and just wanted to track the dominant interactions between pairs so I tossed out the extra bits.

>> awk 'NR>1 && NR<279 {print $1,$3,$5}' hbond_avg.*.out | awk '$3 > .05 {print ;}' > test
>>
>> #make the output to residue# residue# and Hbond%
>> awk -F"_" '$1=$1' OFS=" " test | awk -F"@" '$1=$1' OFS="\t" | awk '{print $2,$5,$7}' | awk '!_[$1,$2]++' > test2a
>> Which will get you the fraction of the trajectory the hbond is present- not quite a 'count' per pair, but since the pairs are all unique, you can just count the number of entries to get total, and do some more awk/sort manipulation of the residue numbers to see what residue is involved in multiple hbonds, etc, which I think is what you want?
